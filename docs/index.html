Verifiable agency for the paperwork you can't glance at.

Wayfinder remembers every document a blind or low-vision person cannot glance at, correlates them into threads, tracks what is still owed and at risk, acts within limits the user sets, and can prove what it did.

Quick start
THREE PILLARS

Abstention, envelope, ledger

Abstention protects the input: it refuses to act on a document it is unsure it read.

The envelope bounds the action: every step is checked against your limits before anything changes.

The ledger proves the output: every decision gets a receipt in a tamper-evident record.

Why it exists

Sighted people verify state by looking. A blind person cannot.

People who cannot see a document are disproportionately targeted by fraud, and routinely disbelieved when they assert what it said. Access to information is not the hard problem — auditable, bounded, accountable agency is.

Reading a letter, bill, or label is an input. The product is the durable, verifiable handler for the paperwork a person cannot glance at. So Wayfinder is built around three ideas no commodity reader provides on its own:

01

Calibrated abstention

Two reads of the same image are compared, and deterministic checks (an account number's checksum, date plausibility, amount sanity) run with no model involved. A low-confidence item is never auto-acted.

02

A bounded envelope

Actions are evaluated against forbidden types, the confidence policy, and your amount thresholds — before any state changes. Refusals are first-class and offer escalation.

03

A verifiable ledger

An append-only, hash-chained log with a receipt for every decision, redacted at write, exportable with a terminal hash anyone can check.

The honest claim. We do not claim bank integration or legal evidentiary weight. We claim the record is internally consistent and tamper-evident: it can prove what the system saw and did, and that it was not altered afterwards.

Quick start

Run it in two terminals

Requires Node.js 22 or newer. No AWS account is needed — without cloud credentials the server uses a deterministic rule-based brain and still works end to end.

npm install
npm run build      # bundle the MCP App view, then compile TypeScript
npm start          # MCP server on http://127.0.0.1:8080
npm run dev:web    # open http://127.0.0.1:5173
# proxies /api and /mcp to the server on :8080

Governance, not vibes

The envelope decides before anything changes

Every action is evaluated against forbidden types, your low-confidence policy, and your amount thresholds — before any state change.

Forbidden

Action types you have disallowed are refused outright, with the reason recorded.

Low confidence

Your policy chooses: ask, refuse, or escalate. Never silently act.

Above your limit

Actions over the amount you set require your confirmation first.

Confirmation is recorded confirmation. The system records that you confirmed. It is not independently verified. An unconfirmed action past its deadline is escalated to your trusted contact — never silently assumed.

Trust

A receipt for every decision

Append-only and hash-chained

Each entry commits to the previous hash, so altering any field breaks every entry after it.

Redacted at write

Sensitive details such as account numbers are redacted before hashing, so the chain never commits to them in the clear.

Verifiable and exportable

Verification recomputes the chain; the exported report carries the terminal hash so anyone can check it independently.

Try verify_ledger

{
  "ok": true,
  "entries": 14,
  "terminal": "9f2c…c41a"
}
// "14 documents, 9 actions, chain verified. Nothing has been altered."

Safety and privacy

No payment execution

A payment handoff prepares an instruction and a SEPA QR payload for you to complete in your own banking app. The ledger records that a handoff was prepared — not that money moved. Invalid account numbers are refused.

No advice, no emergency claims

It reports what a document says. It does not give medical or financial advice, and it does not claim real-time surveillance or emergency response.

Minimal data, local by default

Records and the ledger live in your data directory. Deleting a document purges its content and appends a closed entry that the purge happened, so verification reports it rather than hiding it.

No silent failures

A delivery failure appends delivery_failed and is spoken. With no integrations configured, nothing leaves the process.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Can it pay my bills?

No. It prepares the payment — a spoken instruction and a SEPA QR payload you scan in your own banking app — and records that a handoff was prepared. Wayfinder never executes a payment or holds your credentials.

Is the ledger legal evidence?

We do not claim legal or evidentiary weight. The honest claim is narrower and still useful: the record is internally consistent and tamper-evident, so it can prove what the system saw and did, and that it was not altered afterwards.

What happens when it misreads a document?

Two independent reads are compared and deterministic checks run (an account number's checksum, date plausibility, amount sanity). A disagreement on a critical field marks the document low-confidence, and a low-confidence item is never auto-acted — your policy chooses ask, refuse, or escalate.

Does it need the cloud?

No. Without credentials it runs a deterministic rule brain and reports the image reader as unavailable; correlation, the envelope, the ledger, and every screen still work. Setting a Bedrock model id adds a natural-language brain and a real image reader.

Is it monitoring me?

No. The only monitoring permitted is a channel you own and consent to — your mailbox, your watched folder, your inbound webhook — delivering documents into the same pipeline as a photo. There is no surveillance or emergency-detection claim.

What about fraud?

Correlation and deterministic checks catch the cheap tells: a claimed sender that does not match, a reminder pattern, a demand for an unusual payment instrument, or a failing account checksum. The ledger then makes the reasoning reviewable rather than a black box.

Can I delete a document?

Yes. Deletion removes the stored content and appends a closed entry recording that the content was purged. The chain stays verifiable for everything that remains, and verification reports the purge rather than hiding it.
